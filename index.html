01 / A LITTLE ABOUT ME

I speak softly.
My hands do the exploring.

“I like the feeling of not knowing how to do something yet.”

I’m naturally a little reserved. I often prefer to observe first, notice the small things, and understand how something works before I jump in. But give me a material, a tool, or a new technique, and my curiosity starts asking questions.

Leather art gives that curiosity a place to go. A plain surface can become a world of carved lines, layered colour, and intricate detail. It asks me to be patient, to pay attention, and to keep learning—even when the first attempt isn’t quite right.

This is not a story about being perfect. It’s about discovering what I can make, one careful mark at a time.

03 / WHAT THE CRAFT IS TEACHING ME

Patience is not waiting. It’s paying attention.

Carving and painting on leather has taught me that progress is often quiet. It lives in the line I redraw, the colour I test, and the extra minute I spend understanding a tool. Some days, I make a visible leap. Other days, I simply learn what not to do next time.

I’m learning to trust that kind of progress. Being curious means staying open; being patient means giving an idea enough time to become itself.
